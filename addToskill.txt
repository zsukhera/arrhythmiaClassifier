Your project can use **KANformer as the deep, high-capacity teacher** and **1D-CNN or LSTM as the lightweight
 student**. First, train KANformer normally on the two-channel MIT-BIH ECG input (e.g., a 2-second, 720-sample 
 window per channel) using the true 5-class arrhythmia labels. Then train a standalone 1D-CNN and LSTM as 
 baselines. After that, freeze the trained KANformer and train the student using **knowledge distillation**, where 
 the student learns from both the ground-truth labels and the teacher's soft probability outputs. This lets 
 the teacher transfer information about class similarities and ECG representations that may not be captured by 
 the hard labels alone. The main KD experiment would therefore be **KANformer → 1D-CNN**, with **KANformer → LSTM** 
 as an optional second experiment.


The resulting comparison gives each model a clear purpose: **LightGBM** provides a handcrafted-feature 
baseline, **1D-CNN/LSTM** provide lightweight deep-learning baselines, **KANformer** acts as the high-capacity 
teacher, and **KD-CNN/KD-LSTM** test whether the teacher's knowledge can be transferred into smaller models. 
The most important comparison is not simply accuracy, but the trade-off between **Macro-F1/sensitivity and deployment
 cost** such as parameters, RAM, flash, latency, and energy. The distilled CNN can then be further **quantized/pruned 
 and deployed on an ESP32 with ADS1292**, followed by validation using ECG acquired from the actual hardware.
